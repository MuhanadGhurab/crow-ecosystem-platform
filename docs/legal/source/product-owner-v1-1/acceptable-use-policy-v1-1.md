# Crow Acceptable Use Policy

**Version 1.1 — Effective upon publication**

## 1. Purpose

This Policy protects Crow users, customers, organizations, systems, data, and services.

It applies to all public, requester, client, tenant, ProCrow, API, integration, and runtime activity.

## 2. Authorized use

You may use Crow only:

* for lawful purposes;
* within the access granted to your identity;
* on behalf of organizations you are authorized to represent;
* according to applicable agreements, documentation, and platform instructions.

## 3. Prohibited security activity

You must not, without explicit written authorization:

* access or attempt to access another user's, customer's, or tenant's information;
* bypass authentication, authorization, legal, tenant, or workflow controls;
* test, scan, probe, exploit, or attack Crow systems;
* enumerate accounts, sessions, endpoints, or credentials;
* intercept traffic or authentication material;
* introduce malware, malicious code, or unauthorized automation;
* obtain, disclose, or misuse passwords, tokens, keys, or secrets;
* interfere with monitoring, audit trails, or security controls;
* conceal identity or origin to evade enforcement.

Security testing requires a written scope, approved rules of engagement, and authorized test environment.

## 4. Prohibited service interference

You must not:

* perform denial-of-service activity;
* overload services or evade rate limits;
* disrupt platform availability;
* abuse email, notification, authentication, or recovery systems;
* repeatedly create fraudulent, duplicate, or misleading accounts;
* use bots or scraping tools contrary to documented limits;
* interfere with another user's workflow or service.

## 5. Prohibited data activity

You must not:

* submit data you do not have authority to process;
* disclose confidential or regulated information through unapproved fields;
* upload credentials, private keys, secrets, or malware;
* scrape, harvest, sell, or misuse personal data;
* attempt unauthorized cross-tenant access;
* alter, destroy, or conceal audit evidence;
* evade data-classification, residency, retention, or security requirements.

## 6. Fraud, deception, and harmful conduct

You must not:

* impersonate a person or organization;
* submit fraudulent organization requests;
* falsify ownership, authority, evidence, or approvals;
* harass, threaten, exploit, or deceive users;
* use Crow for unlawful, abusive, discriminatory, or harmful activity;
* violate intellectual-property, privacy, cybersecurity, or contractual rights.

## 7. Discovery and business submissions

Information submitted during requests and discovery must be accurate, lawful, and authorized.

Discovery must not be used to:

* obtain unauthorized architecture details;
* request unlawful surveillance;
* bypass corporate approval;
* impersonate an organization;
* submit third-party confidential information without permission.

Crow may request proof of organizational authority before advancing a request.

## 8. Enforcement

Crow may:

* block an action;
* limit or suspend an account;
* revoke a session;
* quarantine submitted content;
* preserve relevant audit evidence;
* require remediation;
* notify an affected organization;
* report activity where legally required.

Enforcement decisions may consider severity, intent, impact, recurrence, and legal obligations.

## 9. Security research

Researchers must obtain written authorization before testing Crow.

A future vulnerability-disclosure or security-research policy may define:

* authorized targets;
* prohibited techniques;
* reporting channels;
* safe-harbor conditions;
* confidentiality requirements.

Until such a policy is published, no security testing is authorized.

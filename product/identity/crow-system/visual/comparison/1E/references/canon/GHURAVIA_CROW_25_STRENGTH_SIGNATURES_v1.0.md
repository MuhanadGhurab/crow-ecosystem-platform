---

## 1. Core rule: one Crow, one main axis

Every Core Crow Lineage answers one question:

> **What is the one thing this Crow does better than the other four inside its Horizon?**

That answer is its **Strength Signature**.

The Crow may have many supporting qualities, but only one receives dominant visual bandwidth. Its silhouette, Lineage Mark, material logic, and motion personality must all express the same Strength Signature.

This prevents the common failure in which every Crow receives a different pose, color, accessory, mechanism, mood, and special effect but still has no memorable identity.

### Four-part coherence rule

For a design to pass, at least three—and preferably all four—parts must point to the same axis:

1. **Silhouette:** the primary identifier; recognizable as solid black.
2. **Lineage Mark:** a controlled taxonomy marker, never an Evidence Seal or Fusion Signature.
3. **Material and texture logic:** a functional surface behavior, not random visual richness.
4. **Motion personality:** one characteristic way the Crow initiates, reacts, and settles.

If the silhouette fails, markings, materials, glow, and animation cannot rescue the design.

---

## 2. The 80/20 family rule

Within each Horizon:

- **80% shared DNA** comes from the Horizon mother form: species anatomy, at least three of four protected silhouette anchors, center of gravity family, feather-flow grammar, semantic body region, shared material base, and motion rhythm.
- **20% individual specialization** comes from the child's one Strength Signature: dominant silhouette break, Lineage Mark, material variant, and motion personality.

The ratio is a perceptual design rule, not a literal pixel measurement.

The five Crows in one Horizon should be immediately related, but no two should answer the same internal need. Across Horizons, the mother form separates the families; within a Horizon, the Strength Signature separates the five children.

---

## 3. Controlled Lineage Mark system

The user's marking examples are admitted with these governance rules:

- Valid placements include outer chest feathers, outer shoulder, wing edge, eye ring, crest-feather notch, tail edge, and repeated feather pattern.
- A **crest-feather notch** is natural feather geometry; it is never a literal crown or Prestige cue.
- The central sternum and inner bridge zone remain visually quiet enough to host a future governed Fusion Signature.
- Marks use open geometry, cuts, etching, feather grouping, or restrained inlay. They do not look like medals, stamps, certificates, badges, or authority insignia.
- A Lineage Mark identifies taxonomy only. It does not authenticate Evidence.
- Evidence Seals remain a separate authenticated namespace.
- Fusion Signatures remain a separate Major namespace.
- Marks must survive in monochrome. Color may indicate current semantic state but cannot be the mark's identity.
- No mark may imply Trust, Rank, payment, morality, or Prestige.

## 4. Internal role-energy coverage

Each Horizon covers five emotional/behavioral energies so users can recognize affinity, aspiration, complementarity, and team value:

1. **Scout / Sensing** — finds movement, possibility, condition, or change.
2. **Maker / Change** — assembles, tests, transforms, or enables action.
3. **Stabilizer** — protects continuity, integrity, quality, or safe operation.
4. **Specialist / Deep Focus** — develops concentrated excellence in the Horizon's hardest problem.
5. **Coordinator / Higher Order** — connects multiple parts into a coherent result.

These energies are an art-direction coverage check only. They are not new user-facing taxonomy, ranks, personality tests, jobs, or Cross-Wing identities.

## 10. Cross-Wing timing and fusion brief

Final Cross-Wing character art must not be designed before the five mother forms and 25 single-Horizon children pass silhouette, family-grouping, and one-axis recall tests.

### Lock now

- the grammar of fusion;
- parent contribution rules;
- compatibility routes;
- primary-chassis dominance;
- one active Fusion Signature;
- one integrated motion sentence;
- prohibited blends and collision guards.

### Do not lock yet

- final art for all Cross-Wing outcomes;
- full production characters for unapproved Majors;
- decorative surface recipes for every pairing;
- automatic hybrids created from any two completed courses or colors.

Every future Cross-Wing brief must answer exactly four questions before art begins:

1. **What came from Parent A?** — the primary mother form, Lineage capability, and dominant chassis.
2. **What came from Parent B?** — the secondary architecture entering through its semantic region.
3. **What new capability emerged?** — the integrated professional action that neither parent expresses alone.
4. **What stayed impossible?** — unearned capability, prohibited visual inheritance, semantic collision, or behavior the fusion may not claim.

If question 3 has no distinct answer, it is not a Major; it is two traits placed together. If question 4 has no answer, the design has no meaningful boundary and will drift into mashup art.

## 11. Acceptance tests

### Five-second Strength Signature test

After seeing the five members of one Horizon for five seconds, a viewer should be able to assign one distinct strength phrase to each. Exact Lineage names are not required for this early test.

### Solid-black silhouette test

- The Horizon family remains recognizable.
- The child has one dominant silhouette break from its four siblings.
- The design does not depend on accessory, glow, or background.

### Four-part coherence test

- Silhouette, Mark, material, and motion point toward the same axis.
- No part introduces an unrelated second identity concept.
- Supporting personality traits enrich the Strength Signature rather than competing with it.

### 80/20 grouping test

- Unlabeled grayscale portraits can be grouped into the correct five Horizons.
- Within each group, all five children remain distinguishable.
- No child looks more closely related to a Crow in another Horizon than to its own mother form.

### Namespace test

- A Lineage Mark cannot be mistaken for an Evidence Seal.
- A Lineage Mark cannot be mistaken for a Fusion Signature.
- No mark resembles Rank, payment, Prestige, authority, or Trust.

### Motion test

- One still frame implies the intended motion personality.
- The animation remains understandable with glow disabled.
- Reduced motion preserves the same state through pose or slow transition.
- Only one semantic motion sentence dominates.
